# Multiscale Piezoresistivity Simulation Framework for Semiconductors

Piezoresistivity is the change in electrical conductivity of a material under mechanical deformation. Here it is computed end to end, from the DFT ground state to the device response.

The rule for the whole project is that nothing is fitted to a measured resistivity. Every number downstream follows from DFT ground-state quantities and from symmetry. n-type silicon is the benchmark case, checked against Smith (1954).

MSc thesis work, Computational Materials Physics, University of Vienna.

## The chain

```
DFT (VASP 6.3, PAW-PBE)
  -> strained band structures
  -> deformation potentials a, Xi_u, d
  -> phonons (phonopy, finite displacement)
  -> Herring-Vogt semiclassical Boltzmann transport
  -> strain-dependent conductivity tensor sigma(eps)
  -> piezoresistive coefficients pi_11, pi_12, pi_44
  -> FEM device response
```

Each stage is validated on its own before it is allowed to feed the next one. A stage that is not validated is not used.

## Symmetry decomposition

The strain tensor is split into three irreducible channels, and each channel is mapped to a level of transport theory:

- $\Gamma_1$ hydrostatic: shifts the gap, changes the carrier concentration
- $\Gamma_{12}$ uniaxial: splits the $\Delta$ valleys, drives repopulation, produces $\pi_{11}$ and $\pi_{12}$
- $\Gamma_{25'}$ trigonal shear: does not split the $\Delta$ valleys at first order, so $\pi_{44}$ has to come from somewhere else

This split is the architecture of the code, not just bookkeeping. Labels follow BSW notation, as in Yu and Cardona.

## Milestones

### Done and validated

- 1D FEM for steady conduction with constant conductivity, checked against the analytical linear potential profile
- 2D FEM cantilever, mechanical response validated
- Elastic constants $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ from own DFPT run (IBRION=6), not taken from literature
- Effective masses $m_l = 0.959$, $m_t = 0.194$, within about 2% of literature
- Deformation potentials $a = +1.83$ eV, $\Xi_u = 8.89$ eV, $|d| = 5.46$ eV
- Full Herring-Vogt multivalley conductivity tensor with valley repopulation
- $\pi_{11} \approx -85$, $\pi_{12} \approx +39$ (units $10^{-11}$ Pa$^{-1}$), about 80% of Smith's magnitudes
- $\pi_{11}/\pi_{12} = -2$ exactly and independent of $\tau$, which is the internal consistency check on the repopulation channel
- Phonon pipeline: relax -> `phonopy -d` displaced supercells -> force runs -> `--fc-symmetry` -> bandplot, validated against reference dispersion for unstrained Si
- Optical triplet at $\Gamma$ splits 2+1 under trigonal shear, as required
- Phonon dispersions are even in $\varepsilon$, verified numerically on both signs of strain

### Formal results

- $\pi_{44} = 0$ under Herring-Vogt rigid valley masses plus constant-$\tau$ RTA. This is an exact symmetry consequence of the $\Delta$-valley geometry, not a numerical failure. The valley axes sit at the magic angle 54.74 degrees from [111], where the shear energy-shift field vanishes because $P_2(\cos\theta) = 0$.
- The two assumptions are independent. The rigid-mass basis kills the repopulation channel because $\Gamma_{25'}$ has zero multiplicity in the $\Delta$-valley energy-space decomposition. Constant $\tau$ separately kills the scattering channel.

### The 20% gap

The remaining distance to Smith traces to the constant-$\tau$ assumption, not to a pipeline error. This is documented rather than tuned away.

## Two doors for pi_44

Both have to be accounted for before $\pi_{44}$ means anything physically:

- **Mass door**: off-diagonal inverse-mass tensor $M^{-1}$ generated by shear through $\Delta_1$-$\Delta_{2'}$ interband coupling. Reachable inside constant-$\tau$ RTA once the off-diagonal terms are kept. This is the dominant mechanism for n-Si (Kanda and Suzuki 1991), and the closed-form estimate gives about $-13.4 \times 10^{-11}$ Pa$^{-1}$ against Smith's $-13.6$.
- **Scattering door**: state-dependent $\tau(\varepsilon)$ under strain. Needs electron-phonon coupling, so EPW, beyond RTA.

## Conventions and traps

Collected because each of these cost time at least once:

- Use $V_\mathrm{avg}$, not $E_\mathrm{fermi}$, to align band edges across runs
- VASP prints stress in kB with the pressure sign, so negate it for the engineering convention
- $\pm 0.2\%$ strain is the clean derivative window. $\pm 1\%$ already carries higher-order odd contamination
- Extract $\pi$ as the analytic derivative at $\varepsilon = 0$. Fitting over a strain range is biased once $\Xi_u \varepsilon / k_B T$ is not small
- $(S_{11} - S_{12})$ is the strain-to-stress conversion for the uniaxial channel, and it has to be rebuilt for Ge
- The single-exponential $\sigma(\varepsilon) = \sigma_0 \exp(-a \varepsilon_h / 2 k_B T)$ only captures the hydrostatic concentration channel. The $\Xi_u$ channel needs the full multivalley tensor sum
- Phonon frequencies are even in strain, electronic mass cross-terms are expected odd. Useful as a sanity check on any new derivative

## Goal

The long-term goal is a computational framework that connects mechanical deformation, electronic transport, and continuum modelling in piezoresistive materials, with a traceable path from the DFT ground state to a device-level response.
